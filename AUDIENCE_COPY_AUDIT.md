# La La Land Nails audience and copy audit

## Status

The homepage copy and newly added sections were revised on 2026-08-23. Audience groups below are working hypotheses to validate with Bianca and early customers; they are not presented as completed customer research.

## Likely audience groups

### Technique-aware shoppers

Already looking for a European manicure. They expect the technique, provider, price, duration, inclusions, preparation guidance, portfolio proof and a direct route to availability.

### Relationship-led clients

Care who performs the service. They expect a credible provider biography, approved credentials, process or hygiene facts, real work and verified reviews.

### Local, high-intent bookers

Need location, hours, price, availability, contact details, policies and clear confirmation timing before sharing personal information.

### Curious switchers

May not understand the service name. They expect a plain-language explanation, what the appointment includes and a neutral comparison with other salon models.

## What the market teaches visitors to expect

Current specialist, premium and editorial beauty sites consistently pair visual identity with practical service facts. Examples reviewed include [KOVA Social & Beauty](https://kovasocialbeauty.com/), [Polish & Posh](https://www.polishandposh.ca/services-9), [Townhouse](https://townhouse.co.uk/services/), [Paintbox](https://paint-box.com/pages/services), [Vanity Projects](https://www.vanityprojectsnyc.com/), [Selmur](https://www.selmur.com/) and [Dear Sundays](https://dearsundays.com/).

Recurring expectations:

- Service name and location are quickly visible.
- Price, duration and inclusions are explicit.
- Real work, reviews and provider credibility support the promise.
- Booking is direct, with fallback contact information.
- Preparation, removal, cancellation and other policies are available before booking.
- Expressive motion supports the proposition instead of replacing service detail.

## Main audit findings

1. The previous page repeated the same three approved facts across too many sections: European manicures, Bianca Ashley and by appointment.
2. Campaign copy described the website and internal production process instead of answering a client question.
3. The process section did not describe a verified manicure process. It was reframed as the request-to-appointment journey.
4. The comparison was made more neutral and changed from an inaccessible simulated table to labelled comparison cards.
5. Mobile motion typography, persistent booking controls and the campaign composition needed stronger spacing and collision handling.
6. AI artwork needed clearer visible labels so it could not be mistaken for Bianca's client work or results.

## Implemented content strategy

The current sequence is:

1. Exact specialty, provider and appointment request.
2. Compact confirmed service facts.
3. What to expect from request to confirmation.
4. Concise introduction to Bianca.
5. Neutral fit comparison.
6. One disclosed campaign-art interlude.
7. Approved proof, FAQs and policies when supplied.
8. Appointment request form.

## Information still required from the owner

Copy cannot responsibly fill these gaps without approved facts:

- City, neighbourhood or approved service-area label.
- Service price, duration and inclusions.
- Appointment preparation and removal guidance.
- Approved biography, credentials and process or hygiene facts.
- Cancellation, lateness, payment, repair, removal and age policies.
- Fallback contact method, business hours and social profiles.
- Permission-cleared client work, reviews and source attribution.

These remain the highest-impact conversion blockers. The site should continue to avoid search indexing until the launch packet is approved.
